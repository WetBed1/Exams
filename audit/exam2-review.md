# AOSC 105 Test 2: accuracy and study-guide coverage review

Reviewed on November 11, 2026. Bank version 2.0.

The answer keys and map solutions in the original 40-question bank were defensible; the main weakness was uneven direct coverage, plus several explanations needing tighter wording. The expanded exam has 50 original practice questions: 20 multiple choice, 20 written, 10 map skills. Every question is worth 2 points: 40/40/20 = 100.

The uploaded Test 2 Study Guide controls scope: Unit 2 and the winds/upper-air-map portion of Unit 3. The syllabus was reread for course context and its recommended Penn State and NWS resources. Broader syllabus topics do not override the narrower exam guide.

## Material refinements

- Added direct recall of all six phase changes, all ten cloud types, hydrogen bonding, actual-moisture comparison by dew point, hail conditions, lapse-rate definitions, stability/buoyancy definitions, Coriolis latitude, PGF-only map motion, and constant-pressure chart meaning.
- Q11 now tests the density relationship specifically requested by the guide, without requiring extra pressure-versus-altitude reasoning.
- Q26 distinguishes liquid-droplet condensation nuclei from ice nucleation and defines hygroscopic correctly. It does not imply that every dew-forming surface must be hygroscopic.
- Q28 includes the basic sixfold snow-crystal structure and avoids requiring a particular dendritic-growth temperature or treating hail layers as counts of vertical trips.
- Q32 distinguishes a strong low/high circulation from a pressure system that is still strengthening. A mass budget governs pressure tendency.
- Q24 retains the class slide's three fog routes: cooling, evaporation, and their combination. Other references sometimes organize mixing fog separately; that different taxonomy is not imposed on this course question.
- Q33 accepts any four correctly explained lifting mechanisms from the five actually listed in the supplied slide.
- Q38 explains the class's low-base nimbostratus grouping and accepts a justified middle-body classification. It distinguishes cloud base from vertical extent and allows supercooled droplets.
- The lapse-rate calculation retains the stated 10 and 6 C/km assumptions from the course. The saturated rate is not presented as a universal constant, and the environmental rate is kept distinct.
- EDT conversions explicitly use July dates, avoiding confusion with November EST. Station winds use FROM direction; map motion prompts explicitly say TOWARD. The 500-mb contours are heights, not different pressures.

## Verification and limits

- All 50 prompts, option-specific feedback, model answers, rubrics, hints, and common-error explanations were reviewed before the new attempt.
- The four station/radar/isobar/500-mb diagrams were visually checked against their keys.
- The lapse-rate arithmetic was recomputed independently: 16 C at the LCL, 4 C parcel and 2 C environment at 3 km, positive buoyancy, conditionally unstable under the supplied rates.
- All major objectives in the two-page guide are directly assessed. Minor details are included where they support those objectives; this is not a prediction of the professor's exact questions.
- No separate questions were added on fronts/air-mass classification, satellite channels, radiation laws, jet-streak quadrant rules, tornado types, hurricanes, or climate change. Frontal lifting is retained because it is one of the required lifting mechanisms.
- Numerical rates are supplied where needed. Exact cloud altitude boundaries and an exact temperature for a snow-crystal habit are not memorization requirements.
- Written rubrics are practice rubrics, not official instructor grading. The website uses explicit rubric self-checks; chat responses can receive assistant review.
